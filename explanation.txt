We elected to try a variety of models utilizing 5 repeats with 10 fold cross-validation, and selected our final model based on the edition that provided the lowest cross-validated mean square error(MSE). In the same way as our linear model, we used a logarithmic transformation for the Age variable due to the nature of concrete aging doing less once it is cured. Our final model was an ensemble which averaged three models. The first submodel used was cubic regression which creates predictors based on the predictors, their squares, their cubes, and all two and three way interactions. The formulaic ridge penalty helps protect against overfitting of this model. The second submodel used was gradient boosting, which builds upon forest prediction by adapting each tree to the errors of the other, our best edition of this was utilizing fewer large trees as opposed to more small trees. Finally, the neural network creates two “neurons” which weight its input’s sums and work them into a nonlinear function. As this is repeated, the model “learns”. To prevent memorization of datapoints with our relatively smaller dataset, we applied a ridge penalty to the neural weights. Combining these three not only provided the best test MSE, but they help compensate for the errors the other models included are prone to.
